# Food Policy: guide for authors (saved 2026-10-07)

Source: Elsevier *Food Policy* Guide for Authors, as supplied by the user on 2026-10-07. This is a condensed checklist; the full guide is on the journal website.

## Scope and screening (desk-reject criteria)
- **Fit:** the journal covers the economic and social aspects of food policy and favors empirical studies that inform international food policy debates.
- **Desk screening.** Every submission (over 1,200 a year) is checked against these points before review:
  - **Originality:** no overlap with published text. Similarity checks are run on every manuscript.
  - **No overlap on topic and data.** Papers already published on the same topic and data are usually excluded, even when a different subset of the information is used.
  - **Previous rejections:** a paper rejected earlier by a *Food Policy* editor is ineligible unless the editors invite a resubmission in writing.
  - **Contribution to the international food policy debate.** The introduction must give the food policy background and current knowledge.
  - **A specific Policy implications section is required.**
  - **Geographic scope:** a single-country or regional focus is acceptable only if the work is innovative and generalizable, and the generalization must be discussed in the Policy implications section.
- **Quantitative papers.** Data must be representative and good quality. Methods must be transparent and justified, and robustness to the model's assumptions must be discussed.
- **Null results** are acceptable if the design is innovative and rigorous, the findings are discussed critically against other studies, and all relevant robustness checks are done.

## Length
- **Typical article:** 6,000 to 10,000 words for the **complete submission, including the abstract, endnotes, tables, references and appendices.**
- **Policy Comments:** up to 600 words, 10 references and one figure or table; no abstract.
- **Review and Viewpoint articles:** by invitation only.

## Replication (mandatory)
- **Package contents:** data, programs and other materials sufficient to replicate every table and figure, including those in the appendices.
- **Secondary data:** cite the sources and document exactly how every constructed variable was built.
- **Timing:** the package must be public upon acceptance and before publication, and its URL must be reported in the paper.
- **Exceptions:** tell the editors at submission if data are proprietary or replication is impossible; exceptions cannot be raised later.
- **Retention:** materials that cannot be made public must be kept for five years after publication.

## Peer review
- **Inconsistency in the guide:** the Peer review section says the review is *single anonymized*, but the formatting section requires a *double anonymized* format. Prepare for double anonymization to be safe.
- **Double-anonymized format:**
  - submit the title page and the anonymized manuscript as **separate files**;
  - acknowledgements, competing interests and the corresponding author's address go only on the title page;
  - the anonymized manuscript and supplementary files must contain no names, affiliations or acknowledgements.

## Declarations
- **Competing interests:** declarations tool; upload the Word file it produces.
- **Funding:** state funding sources. If none: "This research did not receive any specific grant from funding agencies in the public, commercial, or not-for-profit sectors."
- **Generative AI (required if used).** Add a section before the references titled "Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the manuscript preparation process":
  > During the preparation of this work the author(s) used [TOOL] in order to [REASON]. After using this tool/service, the author(s) reviewed and edited the content as needed and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the published article.
  - AI tools may not be listed or cited as authors.
  - Authors must verify every AI output, including references.
- **CRediT author contributions:** Conceptualization, Data curation, Formal analysis, Funding acquisition, Investigation, Methodology, Project administration, Resources, Software, Supervision, Validation, Visualization, Writing – original draft, Writing – review and editing.
- **Research ethics statement:** required for research involving human participants. It does not apply to this paper, which uses only secondary market and weather data.

## Front matter
- **Title page:** title (concise, no abbreviations); authors' given and family names; affiliations with lower-case superscript letters and full postal address including country; the corresponding author's email; any present address.
- **Abstract:** at most 250 words, standalone. Cover purpose, procedures, main findings with their statistical significance, and conclusions. Avoid references; any that are essential must be given in full.
- **Keywords:** 1 to 7, in English. Avoid multi-word keywords built with "and" or "of".
- **Highlights (required):** 3 to 5 bullets of at most 85 characters each including spaces, submitted as a separate file with "highlights" in the file name.
- **Graphical abstract (encouraged):** at least 531 × 1328 px (h × w), readable at 5 × 13 cm; TIFF, EPS, PDF or MS Office.

## Formatting
- **File format:** Word (.docx) or LaTeX; PDF is not accepted. Word files in a single column. No strikethrough or underline.
- **Sections:** numbered (1, 1.1, 1.1.1). Cross-reference by section number. The abstract is not numbered.
- **Math:**
  - equations as editable text, not images; simple formulae inline;
  - a solidus (X/Y) for small fractions;
  - variables in italics; powers of e written as exp;
  - display equations numbered consecutively in order of reference.
- **Tables:**
  - editable text, not images; placed next to the relevant text or at the end of the article;
  - cited in the text and numbered consecutively, with captions and notes below the table;
  - no vertical rules or shading; used sparingly, without duplicating the text.
- **Figures:**
  - each submitted as a **separate file** (Figure_1, Figure_2, and so on) with a caption;
  - photographs and charts in TIFF, JPG or PNG at ≥ 300 dpi (single column ≥ 1063 px wide; full page ≥ 2244 px);
  - bitmapped line drawings at ≥ 1000 dpi; vector graphics in EPS or PDF;
  - no multi-panel composites unless the panels are meant to be compared, which applies to our Figures 2 and 3;
  - colour figures must be accessible to readers with colour-vision deficiency.
- **Appendices:** labelled A, B, …, with equations (A.1), tables A.1 and figures A.1.
- **Language:** American or British English, not a mix of both.

## References
- **Style:** any consistent style at submission; the journal style is applied at proof.
- **In-text citations:**
  - one author: (Allan, 2020);
  - two authors: (Allan and Jones, 2019);
  - three or more: (Kramer et al., 2023).
- **Reference list:** alphabetical, then chronological; same author and year marked 2020a, 2020b. Include DOIs. Every reference must be real; check AI-generated references.
- **Datasets:** prefix with [dataset] and give author, title, repository, version, year and persistent identifier.
- **Preprints:** mark them as preprints and give the DOI; cite the published version if one exists.
- **Web references:** URL plus the date accessed.

## Data statement
Encouraged: Option A of Elsevier's research-data guidelines (deposit the data in a repository and cite it). A co-submission to *Data in Brief* or *MethodsX* is possible.
